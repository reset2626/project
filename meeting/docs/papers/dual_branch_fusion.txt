 A B S T R A C T
Multivariate time series forecasting is crucial across various critical domains, including weather prediction, 
finance, traffic management, and industrial manufacturing. While deep learning models based on the Trans-
former Neural Network (Transformer) and Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) have recently shown remarkable 
performance, they often suffer from structural uniformity and fixed-scale designs. These limitations hinder 
their ability to fully capture the complex, dynamic, and multi-scale nature of real-world time series data. To 
overcome these limitations, this paper introduces a novel deep learning architecture: the Dual-Branch Multi-
Scale Transformer-MLP (DBMS). The DBMS effectively integrates the Transformer’s capability of modeling 
long-range dependencies and global dynamics with the MLP’s advantage in capturing local patterns and 
short-term fluctuations. Specifically, the model incorporates a coarse-to-fine multi-scale design to perform 
hierarchical feature extraction across multiple temporal resolutions, facilitating more structured temporal 
modeling that captures both global trends and local variations. Furthermore, the proposed dynamic multi-
scale fusion mechanism ensures collaborative perception and robust representation of temporal patterns across 
different scales. Extensive experiments on eight real-world datasets demonstrate that DBMS significantly 
outperforms existing models based solely on Transformer or MLP, achieving state-of-the-art performance with 
superior robustness and stability.
1. Introduction
Long-term time series forecasting (LTSF) plays a critical role in vari-
ous domains, including weather prediction (Lam et al., 2023), financial 
analysis (Zeng et al., 2023b), traffic scheduling (Xu et al., 2020), and 
industrial monitoring (Zamanzadeh Darban et al., 2024). However, 
real-world time series often exhibit strong non-stationarity (Liu et al., 
2022b), periodicity, and multi-scale dynamics, which make it challeng-
ing to model complex dependencies. In recent years, Transformer-based 
architectures have achieved remarkable progress in LTSF due to their 
ability to capture long-range dependencies (Liu et al., 2023; Zhang 
and Yan, 2023; Wang et al., 2025), while MLP-based methods have 
gained increasing attention for their efficiency and structural simplic-
ity (Zeng et al., 2023a). Nevertheless, most existing approaches rely 
on a single architecture or fixed-scale design, limiting their ability to 
simultaneously capture both global and local temporal patterns.
Early studies such as Informer (Zhou et al., 2021), Autoformer (Wu 
et al., 2021), and FEDformer (Zhou et al., 2022) employed complete 
Transformer encoder–decoder structures, mainly enhancing prediction

• We propose a dual-branch architecture that combines a multi-
scale Transformer encoder and a multi-scale MLP, enabling the 
model to simultaneously capture long-term trends and short-term

• We design a fusion module, named HierFusion, based on learnable

that DBMS outperforms existing mainstream methods in terms of

2. Related works
Time Series Forecasting. Traditional statistical models such as 
ETS (Hyndman and Khandakar, 2008; Li et al., 2022), ARMA (Box 
and Jenkins, 1976), and ARIMA (Hyndman and Athanasopoulos, 2018) 
have long served as baselines for time series forecasting. However, 
these methods are based on linear assumptions and perform poorly 
when handling complex real-world data. With the advancement of deep 
learning, methods based on RNN and GNN (Li et al., 2023; Wu et al., 
2020; Miao et al., 2024; Chung et al., 2014; Kieu et al., 2022) were 
proposed to model sequential dependencies, but their limited receptive 
fields restrict their effectiveness for long-term forecasting.
More recently, Transformer-based approaches have achieved re-
markable success due to their ability to capture long-range depen-
dencies. Informer (Zhou et al., 2021), Autoformer (Wu et al., 2021), 
and FEDformer (Zhou et al., 2022) improved forecasting performance 
by enhancing attention mechanisms or incorporating decomposition 
techniques. Crossformer (Zhang and Yan, 2023) and iTransformer (Liu 
et al., 2023) further modeled cross-variable interactions, while
PatchTST (Nie et al., 2022) introduced patching strategies inspired 
by Vision Transformers (Dosovitskiy et al., 2020). Despite their high 
accuracy, these models often suffer from substantial computational 
complexity and limited efficiency.
In parallel, a line of research has demonstrated that simpler MLP-
based methods can also achieve competitive or even superior results. 
DLinear (Zeng et al., 2023a) revealed the surprising effectiveness of 
linear structures. Inspired by the MLP-Mixer (Tolstikhin et al., 2021), 
TSMixer (Chen et al., 2023) and TimeMixer (Wang et al., 2024a) 
employed temporal and channel mixing operations or decomposition-
enhanced MLPs, showing advantages in both stability and efficiency. 
Nevertheless, purely MLP-based models still face challenges in captur-
ing complex global dependencies. Other recent studies have explored 
novel paradigms, such as knowledge aggregation in Transformer-based 
time series models (Xiao et al., 2025) and trajectory encoding augmen-
tation for robust reinforcement learning (Ormancı et al., 2024).
Multi-scale Feature Modeling of Time Series. Multi-scale feature 
extraction has been extensively studied in vision and multimodal learn-
ing (Wang et al., 2021, 2023), and several recent works have extended 
this idea to time series forecasting. Pyraformer (Liu et al., 2022a) 
introduced pyramid attention for multi-scale temporal features, while 
Scaleformer (Shabani et al., 2022) allocated models across different 
resolutions. Pathformer (Chen et al., 2024) designed adaptive paths to

3.1. Model architecture
The overall architecture of DBMS is illustrated in Fig.  2. The model 
is designed around three complementary principles: dual-branch pro-
cessing, hierarchical multi-scale representation, and adaptive fusion.
First, dual-branch design. To address the coexistence of long-
term trends and short-term fluctuations in real-world time series, 
DBMS employs two heterogeneous branches. The first branch applies 
a Fourier-based seasonal-trend decomposition to explicitly separate 
trend and seasonal components. These are then encoded by the Hi-
erarchical Transformer module, which stacks Transformer encoders 
at multiple scales to capture long-range dependencies and periodic 
structures (Vaswani et al., 2017; Nie et al., 2022; Wu et al., 2021; Zhou 
et al., 2021). The second branch, in contrast, processes the raw input 
directly through the Hierarchical MLP module, a hierarchical MLP with 
residual linear blocks. This design leverages the efficiency and strong 
generalization ability of MLPs to capture local patterns and nonlinear 
variations, without requiring explicit decomposition (Tolstikhin et al., 
2021; Chen et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024a; Wu et al., 2022). Such 
complementary inductive biases — global modeling by Transformers 
and local modeling by MLPs — ensure that DBMS is not restricted by 
the assumptions of a single paradigm.
Second, multi-scale representation. Before branch processing, the 
input sequence undergoes hierarchical downsampling to generate mul-
tiple temporal resolutions. Subsequently, each resolution sequence un-
dergoes multiscale processing within different branches, with each 
scale preserving unique semantic information (such as fine fluctuations 
or long-term periodicity) while reducing computational load on high-
capacity modules. For sequences of length 𝑇 , subsampling by a factor of 
𝑠 reduces attention costs from 𝑂(𝑇 2) to 𝑂((𝑇 ∕𝑠)2), enabling manageable 
long-term predictions without sacrificing representational richness.
Finally, adaptive fusion. Outputs from both branches and across 
scales are integrated through the Hierarchical Fusion module. Un-
like static or handcrafted fusion strategies, HierFusion employs learn-
able softmax-normalized weights to generate convex combinations of 
branch- and scale-specific outputs:

Transformers and MLPs, (ii) the representational advantages of hier-

validated through extensive experiments in Section 4.

3.6. Hierarchical fusion
The HierFormer module demonstrates strong global modeling ca-
pability and effectively captures long-range temporal dependencies but 
is less sensitive to local variations. In contrast, the HierMLP module 
focuses on local feature extraction, making it suitable for modeling 
detailed fluctuations and short-term patterns, though it lacks the ability 
to capture long-term dependencies. These two modules complement 
each other in sequence feature extraction, enhancing the model’s repre-
sentational capacity and forecasting performance. The proposed model 
achieves a concise and efficient integration of these two components.

4.1. Data

datasets, including four ETT datasets (ETTh1, ETTh2, ETTm1, ETTm2) 
and the Electricity, Traffic, Weather, and Solar datasets. All datasets 
are publicly available and commonly used in time series forecasting 
research. We followed the same training, validation, and test splits as 
in Wang et al. (2024a). For the ETT datasets, the ratio is 0.6:0.2:0.2,

0.7:0.1:0.2. Additionally, we employed sample entropy (Richman and

higher values indicate greater randomness and lower predictability, 
and lower values correspond to more regular and predictable pat-
terns. Based on this metric, the Electricity and Traffic datasets exhibit 
relatively low predictability, whereas the Weather and Solar datasets 
are comparatively more regular. Table  2 presents detailed statistical 
information for all datasets used in our experiments.

4.2. Baseline
We selected several state-of-the-art models for long-term time series 
forecasting to compare with our proposed approach, including Auto-
former (Wu et al., 2021), FEDformer (Zhou et al., 2022), TimesNet (Wu 
et al., 2022), DLinear (Zeng et al., 2023a), PatchTST (Nie et al., 2022), 
iTransformer (Liu et al., 2023), TimeMixer (Wang et al., 2024a), and 
Time-XL (Liu et al., 2024). These models serve as baselines in our ex-
periments. Some are based on Transformer architectures, while others 
are built upon MLP frameworks, enabling our experiments to clearly 
demonstrate the practical effectiveness of the dual-branch architecture 
proposed in this study. Following standard evaluation protocols in the 
literature, we employ two widely used metrics, mean squared error 
(MSE) and mean absolute error (MAE), to quantitatively assess model 
performance. In addition, motivated by recent studies, we report RMSE 
and RC, and further introduce a composite Time-Series Predictability 
Index (TSPI). TSPI integrates key evaluation components from Zarchi 
et al. (2025) to reflect both prediction accuracy and temporal pattern 
consistency. The detailed computation procedure and complete results 
are provided in Appendix D.
4.3. Setup
All experiments were implemented in PyTorch (Paszke et al., 2019) 
and conducted on NVIDIA Tesla V100 (32 GB) and Tesla T4 (16 GB) 
GPUs, with the random seed fixed at 2021 to ensure reproducibility. 
The lookback window for all models was consistently set to 512. All 
models were trained using the AdamW optimizer (Loshchilov et al., 
2017) with the SmoothL1Loss function. An early stopping strategy was 
employed to prevent overfitting. Specifically, when the validation loss 
failed to decrease for consecutive epochs, training was terminated. The 
patience parameter was set to 12, indicating that training stopped if the 
validation loss did not improve for 12 successive epochs. Additionally, 
whenever the validation loss decreased, the current model parameters 
were saved for final evaluation.
4.4. Multivariate long-term forecasting results
The results of multivariate long-term time series forecasting are 
presented in Table  3. DBMS significantly outperforms existing main-
stream models in most benchmark evaluations and achieves either the 
best or second-best prediction results in the majority of scenarios. In 
the overall comparison across all datasets, DBMS achieves an average 
reduction of 4.7% (MSE) and 4.3% (MAE) compared to the second-best 
model, Timer-XL, demonstrating strong robustness and generalization 
capabilities across diverse forecasting tasks.
This advantage is particularly evident on datasets with high pre-
dictability (i.e., low sample entropy). For instance, on the Weather 
dataset, DBMS reduces prediction errors by 7.1% (MSE) and 5.7% 
(MAE) compared to the second-best model, PatchTST, and achieves 
average reductions of 25.5% (MSE) and 21.9% (MAE) compared to the 
remaining models. On the Solar dataset, DBMS also surpasses Timer-
XL, reducing errors by 6.4% (MSE) and 8.8% (MAE), and shows an 
average reduction of 16.4% (MSE) and 24.1% (MAE) relative to other 
competing models.
Even on challenging datasets with low predictability (i.e., high 
sample entropy), DBMS continues to deliver competitive results. On 
the ETTm1 dataset, it reduces error by 3.6% (MSE) and 3.1% (MAE) 
compared to PatchTST, and achieves average improvements of 17.2% 
(MSE) and 12.3% (MAE) compared to the other models. Similarly, on 
the ETTm2 dataset, DBMS outperforms PatchTST by 3.1% (MSE) and 
3.2% (MAE), while achieving average reductions of 22.7% (MSE) and 
13.0% (MAE) over the remaining baselines.
4.5. Ablation study
To assess the contribution of each component, we conducted abla-
tion studies on DBMS across eight benchmark datasets with prediction 
lengths of {96, 192, 336, 720}. In each setting, all hyperparameters 
were fixed while a specific module was removed: Ablation A represents 
the full DBMS, B removes HierFormer, C removes HierMLP, D replaces 
HierFusion with direct addition, E removes FSTD, and F substitutes the 
multi-scale design with a single-scale variant. As shown in Table  4, 
removing any module degrades prediction performance, with a more 
pronounced decline at longer horizons (336 and 720). This indicates 
that the synergistic interaction between the Transformer and MLP 
branches, together with multi-scale decomposition and dynamic fusion, 
is crucial for robust performance. At shorter horizons (96 and 192), 
differences are minor, suggesting weaker dependence on individual 
modules. Overall, the complete DBMS consistently achieves the best 
results, validating the effectiveness of each component.

4.7. Seasonal trend decomposition analysis
To provide a more intuitive understanding of the performance 
differences between the proposed FSTD-based seasonal-trend decom-
position method and the conventional moving average approach, we 
conducted a comparative ablation study, as shown in Fig.  7. While 
the traditional moving average decomposition effectively smooths time 
series, its ability to identify periodic components remains implicit and 
highly sensitive to the choice of kernel size, limiting its capacity to 
accurately extract distinct seasonal patterns. In contrast, the proposed 
FSTD method, which employs a Fourier-based low-pass filter, enables 
more precise and efficient separation of trend components at prede-
fined frequencies and substantially enhances the model’s ability to 
capture periodic structures. Notably, the performance gap between the 
two methods becomes more pronounced at longer prediction horizons 
(e.g., 336 and 720), where FSTD achieves clearer and more stable 
extraction of seasonal trends, whereas the moving average approach 
produces smoother but less distinct representations.

4.8. Double-branch seasonal trend ablation experiment
To validate the rationale for applying seasonal-trend decomposition 
before the HierFormer module but not before the HierMLP module, we 
conducted an ablation study, as shown in Fig.  8. Column A represents 
the case where seasonal-trend decomposition is applied only before 
the HierFormer module, whereas Column B applies it only before 
the HierMLP module. Column C applies seasonal-trend decomposition 
before both the HierFormer and HierMLP modules, while Column D 
omits the decomposition entirely.
The results support the structural design of our model. This may be 
attributed to the fact that, although the HierFormer module can capture 
complex dependencies, it is sensitive to interference when trend and 
seasonal components are mixed in the original sequence, which can 
reduce modeling efficiency. Applying seasonal-trend decomposition be-
forehand disentangles these components, enabling HierFormer to focus 
more effectively on long-term trends or cyclical seasonal information, 
thereby improving stability and prediction accuracy. In contrast, the 
HierMLP branch primarily captures local patterns within the input win-
dow, concentrating on short-term structures. Given its limited capacity

4.9. Cutoff ratio analysis of low-pass filters
We conducted an ablation study on the core hyperparameter cut-

determining the proportion of low-frequency components retained for 
trend reconstruction, while the remaining high-frequency components 
are treated as the seasonal component. Notably, on the ETTh1 dataset, 
different cutoff_ratio settings lead to substantial variations in 
prediction performance, whereas on the Weather dataset, the impact 
is comparatively minor.

4.10. Analysis of the average number of downsampling layers
To evaluate the impact of multi-level downsampling, we varied the 
number of downsampling layers from 0 to 4 while keeping all other pa-
rameters constant. As shown in Fig.  11, we report the MSE performance 
on the ETTh1 and Weather datasets. The results indicate that apply-
ing downsampling to the original data yields significant performance 
gains, particularly for longer prediction horizons (e.g., 336 and 720). 
This improvement arises because long-term forecasting generally re-
quires capturing coarse-grained temporal trends over an extended time 
span, whereas fine-grained fluctuations may introduce additional noise. 
Downsampling filters out high-frequency components and redundant 
details, allowing the model to extract more stable long-term dynamic 
structures. Notably, as the number of downsampling layers increases, 
the performance improvement begins to plateau, suggesting that a 
moderate number of downsampling stages enhances feature abstraction 
while avoiding information loss.

integration of information without introducing excessive complexity.

To ensure statistical reliability of the results, this paper reports

fluctuation. These results further validate the statistical reliability and 
robustness of the model’s predictions.
5. Conclusion
This paper proposes a dual-branch multi-scale framework (DBMS), a 
novel architecture that combines the strengths of Transformer encoders 
and multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs) to enhance time series forecast-
ing. Experiments demonstrate that the dual-branch structure facilitates 
synergistic interactions between the two architectures. Each branch 
employs a multi-scale design to capture sequence features more com-
prehensively. To integrate the resulting cross-scale features, we in-
troduce a learnable fusion module that adaptively weights features 
at different scales, achieving more effective integration than conven-
tional approaches. Extensive evaluations show that DBMS delivers 
superior prediction accuracy and robustness across multiple benchmark 
datasets.
Although DBMS demonstrates advantages in multiscale time se-
ries forecasting, certain limitations remain. Multiscale processing in-
evitably increases computational overhead, leaving room for further 
optimization in terms of inference efficiency and memory consumption. 
Furthermore, this study primarily relies on publicly available bench-
mark datasets for evaluation, and its generalization capability in more 
complex or dynamically changing real-world scenarios requires further 
validation.
Looking ahead, time series forecasting faces several emerging trends 
and challenges. On one hand, developing more efficient feature fu-
sion mechanisms, lightweight architectures, and leveraging frequency-
domain information may become key directions for improving pre-
diction performance. On the other hand, effectively handling non-
stationary time series, enhancing cross-domain generalization, and 
achieving a balance between efficiency and accuracy remain central 
challenges to be addressed. These issues not only provide valuable 
guidance for the future development of DBMS but also open new 
possibilities for advancing time series forecasting research.
