Abstract
  A new method to construct nonparametric prediction intervals for nonlinear time series data is proposed. Within the framework
of the recently developed sieve bootstrap, the new approach employs neural network models to approximate the original nonlinear
process. The method is ﬂexible and easy to implement as a standard residual bootstrap scheme while retaining the advantage of
being a nonparametric technique. It is model-free within a general class of nonlinear processes and avoids the speciﬁcation of a
ﬁnite dimensional model for the data generating process. The results of a Monte Carlo study are reported in order to investigate the
ﬁnite sample performances of the proposed procedure.

1. Introduction

   In the analysis of time series data, the estimation of forecast intervals based on an observed sample path of the
process is a key issue. If the process is linear and the distribution of the error process is known, the methodology
is well developed but, for departures from the true underlying distribution, the prediction intervals perform poorly
(Thombs and Schucany, 1990). In this latter case, several distribution free alternatives based on the bootstrap and
on the speciﬁcation of ﬁnite dimensional linear models have been proposed (Grigoletto, 1998; Pascual et al., 2006).
More recently, Alonso et al. (2002) proposed an AR-Sieve bootstrap approach with the interesting property of being
model-free within the class of linear processes. The approach, which does not impose the choice of a particular model,
is effective for linear and “weakly” nonlinear processes. Unfortunately, it is not consistent if the underlying process
does not admit an AR(∞) representation.
   In this paper we propose a new method to construct nonparametric prediction intervals for nonlinear time series data.
Our approach is in the spirit of the sieve bootstrap proposed by Bühlmann (1997), following the scheme of Alonso
et al. (2002) for linear processes, but it uses the class of neural network models to approximate the original nonlinear
process (Giordano et al., 2005).

2. Neural network sieve bootstrap forecast intervals

   The sieve bootstrap is based on the idea of sieve approximation and it should be seen as a nonparametric scheme.
Following Bühlmann (1999), a general stochastic process {Yt , t = 0, ±1, ±2, . . .} can be approximated by a family
of models {Mr , r = 1, 2, . . .} equipped with a model selection rule. Clearly, a key issue is the selection of a proper
parametric model family.
   For general stationary categorical processes, the variable length Markov chain has been used (Bühlmann, 1999).
For linear processes, a straightforward choice is the class of AR(p) models with ﬁnite unknown p, assuming that some
consistent estimator is available (Bühlmann, 1997). The latter approach performs better than other bootstrap techniques
if the data generating process is linear, representable as an AR(∞) process.
    If the model is nonlinear, the AR-Sieve bootstrap is not consistent and its success is related to the closeness of the
underlying process to the AR(∞) representation. Bühlmann (1997, 2002) shows, by simulation, that for exponential
autoregressive models of order 2 and for some classes of self-exciting threshold autoregressive models, the AR-Sieve
bootstrap estimation exhibits a bias which does not decrease with increasing sample size. Therefore, an alternative
approach for nonlinear data generating processes is needed.

4. Concluding remarks

  A new bootstrap approach to estimate the prediction intervals for nonlinear time series has been presented. The
proposed bootstrap technique, which is based on the use of artiﬁcial neural networks in a sieve bootstrap scheme, has
the advantage of being ﬂexible and easy to implement as a standard residual bootstrap technique. In any case it has a
nice nonparametric property being model-free within a general class of nonlinear processes, avoiding the speciﬁcation
of a ﬁnite dimensional model for the data generating process.
   The simulation experiment, carried out to study the ﬁnite sample performances of the proposed method, shows that
for nonlinear models the NN-Sieve bootstrap outperforms the AR-Sieve in terms of both mean and variability of the
observed coverage. For linear and weak nonlinear models the two approaches seem to be equivalent.
